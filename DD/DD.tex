\chapter{Introduction}
    In the realm of IT, programming stands as a significant challenge, making challenges an invaluable tool for students to gain the drive and opportunity to delve into, comprehend, and enjoy various topics.
    The CodeKataBattle platform embodies this reality by transforming learning into a real challenge.

    \section{Purpose} \label{purpose}
    Derived from the karate practice of ``kata'', the CodeKataBattle project mirrors the concept of iterative improvement through repetition.
    In the context of competitive programming, it leverages this idea by offering a multitude of problems (referred to as ``code katas``) for students to solve.
    The core principle lies in fostering learning through repeated attempts and learning from mistakes, thus emphasizing the trial-and-error approach.

    The platform serves two main user groups.
    On one hand, individuals seeking to enhance their programming skills and engage in competition with friends or colleagues.
    On the other hand, educators, either from the CKB owner company or enthusiastic public users with a passion for
    teaching informatics.

\section{Scope}
    CodeKataBattle's domain is centered on providing an exclusive platform where students can actively participate in programming challenges.
    Simultaneously, educators are empowered to curate and share various kata problems aligned with their educational goals.

    Let's now shortly introduce the main goals of the CKB project.
    \begin{description}
        \item[G1] \textbf{Allow educators to create Kata Tournaments}
            \\ 
            Educators should be able to define the details of the tournament, such as its name and description.
        \item[G2] \textbf{Allow educators to create Kata Battles for a specific Kata Tournament}
            \\
            Educators should be able to upload code katas, set the minimum and maximum number of students per group,
            define registration and submission deadlines, and configure scoring aspects for each battle.
        \item[G3] \textbf{Allow educators to evaluate students automatically via the system and optionally via a manual scoring}
            \\
            The system should automatically evaluate functional aspects, timeliness, and source code quality of the students' solutions.
            Additionally, there should be an option for educators to perform manual evaluations and assign personal scores based
            on their assessment.
        \item[G4] \textbf{Allow students to subscribe to Kata Tournaments available on the CKB platform}
            \\
            Students should have the ability to see Kata Tournaments infos and subscribe.
        \item[G5] \textbf{Allow students to join Kata Battles of a certain Kata Tournament}
            \\
            Students should be able to form teams or join battles individually, respecting the minimum and maximum number
            of students per group set by educators.
        \item[G6] \textbf{Allow students to compete in specific Kata Tournament}
            \\
            It encompasses the entire process, from coding to submitting solution and ultimately competing for scores and rankings.
        \item[G7] \textbf{Provide notifications about major events}
            \\
            This goal ensures that users stay informed about key activities and developments.
    \end{description}

\section{Definitions, Acronyms, Abbreviations}

Here's a list of useful definitions necessary to a comprehensive understanding of the CodeKataBattle RASD document

\subsection{Definitions}\label{definitions}

\begin{description}
    \item[Kata Tournament :] A competition organized by educators on the CKB platform, where students
        can participate in various Kata Battles.
    \item[Kata Battle :] A competition organized by an educator associated to a Kata Tournament, where students
        compete with each other by programming solutions to code katas.
    \item[Organizer :] An educator who possesses the permission to upload KB for a specific tournament.
    This authorization is granted when the educator has been invited by the creator of the tournament.
    \item[Code Kata :] A programming problem associated with each Kata Battle uploaded by an Organizer.
        It consists in a description of the problem, a software project, build automation scripts and test cases.
    \item[Team :] A group of one or more students taking part to a Kata Battle.
    \item[Consolidation Stage :] In this phase, the Kata Battle's Organizer will manually assess and evaluate the submissions made by students.
        If no manual evaluation is required, it does not exist.
        This phase commences upon the conclusion of a KB and extends until the educator decides to finalize it.
    \item[Tournament Rank :] The position of a student in a tournament in comparison to others based on the sum of the scores
    he obtained in each Kata Battle of that Kata Tournament.
    \item[Battle Rank :] The position of a team in a battle in comparison to others based on their score.
    \item[Score :] The numerical representation, between 0 and 100, of the students' performance in a battle,
        calculated taking into consideration every evaluation factor in a proper way.
    \item[Major Event :] Events that take place in the CKB platform and need to be notified to the specific students.
\end{description}

\chapter{Architectural Design}

The subsequent section delineates the architecture of the CKB system, progressing from a high-level overview to a more detailed perspective.
Our emphasis will primarily be on outlining the system's roles, specifying how components acquire application functionalities,
and detailing their deployment and runtime usage.
Additionally, this section provides a clear definition of the architectural patterns employed for deploying all identified
components, along with other design decisions made during the architectural design process.

\section{Overview}
CKB has been designed as a 4-tier service-oriented architecture, and follow the SOA style instead of a monolithic design.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics*[width=\textwidth]{High-Level-Overview}
\caption{Informal High Level Overview of the CKB System}
\centering
\label{fig:high_level_overview}
\end{figure}

The 4-tier subdivision is composed as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item[1°] \emph{The Client Presentation tier} : User interface components for a responsive and interactive experience.
    \item[2°] \emph{The Server Presentation tier} : Manages user requests, processes data, and orchestrates information flow.
    \item[3°] \emph{The Business tier} : Contains core logic and functionality, including independent services.
    \item[4°] \emph{The Data tier} : Manages data storage and retrieval through a DBMS\@.
\end{itemize}

For a better explanation see section ~\ref{architectural_styles}

Furthermore, the CKB system engages with external actors crucial for its operation:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{GitHub Actions or Other Services} : The system will be notified of team's new commits onto their main branches.
    \item \emph{GitHub} : CKB is required pull the updated code and send it to the automatic evaluation process.
    \item \emph{Email Provider} : CKB will need to send confirmation emails to new users, and a stream of notification emails.
\end{itemize}

\section{Component view}

This part focuses on the inner architecture of each subsystem, pointing out the connections between all the inner
components and the ones with any external component which has to interact with this system.

Detailed descriptions:
\begin{description}
    \item[Web Server - ] The server component that connect the website interface to our application server.
    It handles user requests and interfaces with other backend services.
    \item[AuthenticationService - ] Responsible for user authentication,  ensures secure access to the platform.
    It verifies user credentials and issues authentication tokens for authorized access to other services.
    \item[EducatorService - ] Offer functionalities to create and manage KTs and KBs.
    Educators can define problem statements, set deadlines, and evaluate student submissions.
    \begin{description}
        \item[KTManagerService - ]  Focuses on the management of Kata Tournaments.
        It facilitates tournament creation, organization, and coordination, ensuring smooth collaboration between educators and organizers.
        \item[KBManagerService - ] Handles the administration of Kata Battles.
        Educators utilize this service to set up coding challenges, define battle rules, and manage teams' participation.
    \end{description}
    \item[StudentService - ] Enables students to form teams for KataBattles and participate in KataTournament.
    \item[InfoService - ] Acts as a centralized hub for platform information.
    It provides relevant data to users, including tournament and battle details, user profiles, and general platform statistics.
    \item[SubmissionService - ] Responsible for handling code submissions, ensures the secure and efficient processing of student submissions during Kata Battles.
    Provides an external API to submit new code solutions, and it interfaces with the AutomaticEvaluation component.
    \item[AutomaticEvaluation - ] Automate the evaluation of code submissions.
    Static analyzer give to educators the possibility to check code quality, while dynamic evaluation executes code against test cases to determine correctness and efficiency.
    Requires an external API to GitHub to allow pull actions.
    \item[NotificationController - ] Manages the delivery of notifications to users.
    It handles event-driven communication, informing users about important updates, upcoming deadlines, or results.
    Requires an external API to send e-mail.
    \item[Model - ] Represents the data model of the platform.
    It defines the structure of entities such as users, teams, tournaments, and submissions, facilitating data storage and retrieval.
    It's connected externally with the DBMS\@.
    \item[DBMS - ] Responsible for storing and managing the platform's data.
    It ensures data integrity, accessibility, and scalability, supporting efficient retrieval and storage of information for all platform components.
\end{description}

\section{Deployment view}

This section illustrates the deployment diagram for the system, outlining the intended environments, tools, and communication protocols essential for constructing the system.

Before delving into the deployment view, it's important to remember that the system has been designed with a service-oriented architecture.
For this reason the Web, Application, and Database Servers will be represented by a single instance but may potentially represent a greater number of instances, as the service-oriented architecture allows us to deploy each service on one or more servers in parallel.
As of now we can only estimate the system's size and, for this reason, the decision on the actual dimensions of the system, that is to say the actual number of servers and VMs needed, will be taken during development.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics*[width=0.7\textwidth]{Deployment}
\caption{Component View of the CKB system}
\centering
\label{fig:component_view}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Client}: a generic Client device, that is to say a computer or a mobile phone.
    Runs a web browser software that renders the web pages received by the Web Server.
    \item \textbf{Web Server}: are generic Server computer that host a Web Server execution environment (Tomcat) and receive Client requests.
    If the Client request needs it, the Web Servers will forward the request to an Application Server.
    \item \textbf{Application Servers}: are generic Server computers that host linux virtual machines.
    Each virtual machine will contain an execution environment capable of hosting Spring Applications (explained in detail in section \ref{design_decisions}).
    \item \textbf{Database Servers}: are generic Server computers that host linux virtual machines.
    Each virtual machine will contain a DBMS, allowing Application Servers to retrieve the needed data via TCP/IP.
    \item \textbf{Load Balancer}: are servers that handle incoming HTTPS requests and balance the workload among the Web Servers, consiquently balancing the Application Servers.
   \item \textbf{Firewall}: are devices that monitor the incoming HTTPS requests, trying to spot if those requests are potentially dangerous to the system and eventually ignoring them.
\end{itemize}

\section{Selected architectural styles and patterns} \label{architectural_styles}
\begin{description}
    \item[4-tier architecture]
    The CKB platform adopts a 4-tier architectural structure to enhance modularity, scalability, and maintainability.
    This structure is composed of the following tiers:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[1°] \emph{Client Presentation Tier} encompasses the user interface components, providing a responsive and interactive experience for users.
        Is intended as a thin client, so it will handle as little presentation logic as possible.
        \item[2°] \emph{Server Presentation Tier} manages the interaction between the client and the business logic.
        It handles user requests, processes data, and orchestrates the flow of information.
        \item[3°] \emph{Business Tier} contains the core logic and functionality of the platform.
        It encapsulates services such as EducatorService and StudentService.
        By organizing functionality into a dedicated business tier, the platform achieves a modular and maintainable design.
        \item[4°] \emph{Data Tier} focus on data storage and retrieval.
        The Database Management System manages the persistence of data, supporting the overall reliability and efficiency of the platform.
        This tier ensures the integrity and availability of essential information, and is accessible by every service in the Business tier
    \end{itemize}
    The subdivision of the Presentation logic between Client and Server has been conceived to lighten as much as possible the client computational load.

    \item[Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA)]
    The CKB platform adopts a Service-Oriented Architecture to foster flexibility, scalability, interoperability and seamless integration of services.
    Each major functionality, such as authentication, educator management, and student interaction, is encapsulated within independent services.
    This allows for distributed development, making it easier to scale and update specific components without affecting the entire system, while allowing independent development workflows for each service.
\end{description}

\chapter{Implementation, Integration and Test Plan}
After having described all the components of the system and their interconnections, this chapter describes how
these should be implemented, integrated, and tested in order to have a working system.
In general, the method followed for building up the system is to first start with a bottom-up approach
to create the component that directly interacts with the database, to have a fully working data retrieval
and management.
Then a team approach is used to develop all the other components in parallel, dividing the work by functionality.
This approach is possible due to the modular architecture, and is recommended to ensure interoperability and modularity.

\subsection{Implementation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item[Data Tier] Implement the DBMS and associated structures to store and manage data effectively.
    \item[Business Tier] Develop core business logic and services concurrently to allow multiple teams to work on distinct services.
    Emphasize a modular approach for independent development and testing.
    \item[Server Presentation Tier] Develop server-side components for handling user requests.
    Ensure early communication protocols are in place for integration testing.
    \item[Client Presentation Tier] Develop user interface components concurrently.
    Collaborate with the server-side team for early integration points.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Integration}
\begin{itemize}
    \item[Client - Server] Integrate the user interface with server-side components.
    Establish communication protocols for efficient data exchange.
    \item[Server - Business] Integrate server-side components with the business logic.
    Perform early integration tests to identify and address potential issues.
    \item[Business - Data] Integrate business logic with the data tier.
    Verify the consistency of data flow and handling across different components.
    \item[External Actor] Confirm proper engagement with external actors, including GitHub for pull operations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Testing}
\begin{itemize}
    \item[Unit] Implement unit tests for services, components, and database operations  to verify their correctness independently.
    \item[Integration] Conduct integration tests to verify seamless interaction between different tiers.
    \item[System-Level] Perform system-level tests to evaluate overall system performance, user interactions, and data flow.
\end{itemize}
